Part 0 · 15 minutes

Before you start

Gather everything first, so you don't have to stop halfway.

Who you need

PersonWhy

The owner (Light)Signs in to the Netflex office (netflexhq.com/admin) for Parts 8, 10 and 11, and chooses and keeps the passwords.

What you need

  • The office laptop (HP, Windows 11) and its charger
  • A network cable long enough to reach from the laptop to the PoE switch (the ONV box)
  • A straightened paper clip or SIM-tray pin, to press the reset holes on the access points
  • A ladder, to reach the access points in the hallways
  • A phone for testing (any Android or iPhone)
  • The owner's phone with the authenticator app, to sign in to the Netflex office
  • Working internet from Starlink

Passwords you will create

You will make three new passwords. Choose each one together with the owner. Write them down only in the owner's password manager, or on paper kept in a sealed envelope in the owner's safe.

Part 1 · 20 minutes

Get the laptop ready

The laptop becomes the "brain" of the Wi-Fi. It must stay on, awake and connected by cable.

A. Plug it in by cable

  1. Put the laptop somewhere safe in the office, near a power socket, where it won't be moved or closed by accident.
  2. Plug the laptop's charger into the surge protector (not straight into the wall).
  3. Plug one end of the network cable into the laptop's network port (the wide square socket on the side). Plug the other end into a free port on the PoE switch, the ONV box the access points are connected to.
  4. Turn the laptop's Wi-Fi off: click the network icon at the bottom-right of the screen (next to the clock), then click the Wi-Fi tile so it turns grey.
Check

The network icon at the bottom-right now shows a small screen with a cable, not Wi-Fi bars. Open Chrome or Edge and visit google.com. It should load. If not, check both ends of the cable are clicked in firmly.

B. Stop it from sleeping

  1. Click Start (the Windows logo), then Settings (the gear).
  2. Click System, then Power & battery (it may be called Power).
  3. Open Screen and sleep (or Screen, sleep & hibernate timeouts).
  4. Set "When plugged in, put my device to sleep after" to Never. You may leave the screen turning off; that's fine.

C. Keep it on when the lid is closed

  1. Click Start, type control panel and open Control Panel.
  2. Click Hardware and Sound, then Power Options.
  3. On the left, click Choose what closing the lid does.
  4. Next to "When I close the lid", under Plugged in, choose Do nothing. Click Save changes.

D. Stop Windows restarting at busy times

  1. Settings → Windows Update → Advanced options → Active hours.
  2. Set it to Manually, from 6:00 AM to 11:00 PM. Windows then only restarts for updates at night.
Part 1 done: laptop on cable, never sleeps, lid can close.

Part 2 · 10 minutes

Install Node.js

Node.js is free software from the Node.js Foundation. The Netflex lodge link (Part 10) needs it to run.

  1. On the laptop, open Chrome or Edge and go to nodejs.org.
  2. Click the big green button that says Download Node.js (LTS). LTS means the stable version: use that one, not "Current".
  3. When the download finishes, open the file (it ends in .msi). If Windows asks "Do you want to allow this app to make changes?", click Yes.
  4. In the installer, click Next, tick I accept the terms, click Next, Next, Next.
  5. On the page "Tools for Native Modules", leave the box unticked. Click Next, then Install, then Finish.

Check it worked

  1. Click Start, type powershell, and open Windows PowerShell. A blue or black window with text appears. This is where you type commands.
  2. Type node --version and press Enter.
PS C:\Users\Office> node --version v22.12.0
Check

You see a line starting with v and a number of 20 or higher (for example v22.12.0 or v24.1.0). Close the window.

If it says "node is not recognized"

Close PowerShell, restart the laptop, and try the check again. If it still fails, run the installer again.

Part 2 done: Node.js installed.

Part 3 · 20 minutes

Reset the 3 access points

The access points are the white discs in each hallway. They're still set up the old way (each one on its own, network name "Omada"). Resetting them lets the controller take them over.

Wi-Fi will be off in the lodge for about an hour

From here until Part 6, residents have no Wi-Fi. Pick a quiet time and warn them.

Why do the check first?

Each access point should have a label underneath saying EAP115 and Ver: 4.x. If any says Ver: 1 or Ver: 2, stop and send a photo of that label to Netflex: it may not work with the new controller.

Do this for each of the 3 access points, one at a time:

  1. Climb the ladder safely. Look at the access point: its light should be on (it gets power through its cable from the PoE switch).
  2. Find the small RESET hole, on the side or near the cable socket.
  3. Push the paper clip into the hole until you feel a click. Hold it in for 10 seconds.
  4. Let go when the light starts flashing. The access point restarts by itself. Leave it; it takes about 2 minutes.
  5. Do not unplug its cable.
Check

After about 2 minutes, on your phone, look at the list of Wi-Fi networks: the old "Omada" network may disappear or change. That's expected. All three should have a steady light again.

Part 3 done: all 3 access points reset.

Part 4 · 30 minutes

Install the Omada Controller

The Omada Controller is free software from TP-Link (the maker of the access points). It runs on the laptop and manages all three access points together.

A. Download and install

  1. On the laptop, open Chrome or Edge and search for Omada Software Controller download. Open the result on omadanetworks.com (TP-Link's official site).
  2. Find the newest Omada Software Controller for Windows (version 5.x or 6.x) and click to download it. It's a large file; wait for it to finish.
  3. If it downloaded as a .zip file: right-click it → Extract All → Extract. Then open the folder.
  4. Double-click the installer (its name starts with Omada_SDN_Controller or Omada_Network_Application and ends in .exe). Click Yes if Windows asks.
  5. Click Next on each screen, leaving everything as it is, then Install, then Finish.

B. Start it for the first time

  1. Open Omada Controller from the desktop icon or the Start menu. A small window appears and starts it. The first start takes up to 5 minutes.
  2. If a Windows Firewall box appears, tick Private networks and click Allow access.
  3. When it's ready, the small window has a button Launch a Browser to Manage the Network. Click it. (You can also type https://127.0.0.1:8043 into Chrome.)
  4. The browser shows "Your connection is not private". This is normal for the controller on this laptop. Click Advanced, then Proceed to 127.0.0.1 (unsafe).

C. The setup wizard

  1. Controller name: ND Lodge. Country: Nigeria. Time zone: (UTC+01:00) West Central Africa / Africa/Lagos. Click Next.
  2. If it asks to configure devices or Wi-Fi now, click Skip. We do that in Parts 5 and 6.
  3. Admin account: username admin (or the owner's choice), and the Omada admin password from Part 0. Enter an email the owner reads, for password recovery.
  4. If it offers Cloud Access or a TP-Link ID, you can turn it off or skip it. Netflex doesn't need it.
  5. Click Finish, then sign in with the admin username and password.

D. Start it automatically

  1. Press the Windows key and R together. Type shell:startup and press Enter. A folder opens.
  2. Find the Omada Controller icon on the desktop. Hold the Ctrl key and drag the icon into that folder, to copy it there.
After any restart, sign in to Windows once

The controller starts when someone signs in to Windows. Stick a note on the laptop: "After a restart, sign in. Don't close the Omada window." If nobody does, the owner gets an email alert from Netflex.

Part 4 done: controller installed, wizard finished, starts with Windows.

Part 5 · 20 minutes

Add the access points

Where: Omada Controller in the browser on the laptop

  1. In the controller, click Devices in the left menu.
  2. You should see the 3 access points (model EAP115) with the status Pending. If not all 3 are there, wait 2 minutes and refresh the page.
  3. Click Adopt next to each one (or Batch Adopt to do all three).
  4. Wait. The status changes to Adopting, then Provisioning, then Connected. This takes 2 to 5 minutes.
  5. If a device shows an upgrade arrow or says new firmware is available, click it and choose Upgrade. Wait until it says Connected again (about 5 minutes). Don't unplug anything while it upgrades.
  6. Name each one so you know which is which: click the access point, then the pencil next to its name, and call it Floor 1, Floor 2, Floor 3. (To find which is which, compare the MAC address on its label with the one shown.)
Check

Devices shows all 3 access points as Connected, named Floor 1, Floor 2 and Floor 3.

If an access point says "Adopt failed" or asks for a username and password

Try username admin and password admin (the factory default). If that fails, reset it again (Part 3) and wait 3 minutes.

Part 5 done: all 3 access points connected.

Part 6 · 15 minutes

Create the two Wi-Fi networks

Where: Omada Controller → Settings (gear, bottom-left) → Wired & Wireless Networks → WLAN

A. The residents' network

  1. Click Create New Wireless Network (or + Add).
  2. Fill it in exactly like this:
    SettingValue
    Network Name (SSID)ND Lodge Wi-Fi
    Band2.4 GHz and 5 GHz (both ticked; the EAP115 only uses 2.4 GHz)
    Guest NetworkOn (keeps residents' phones from seeing each other)
    SecurityNone, no password. The sign-in page protects it.
    SSID BroadcastOn
  3. Click Apply (or Save).

B. The staff network

  1. Click Create New Wireless Network again.
  2. Fill it in:
    SettingValue
    Network Name (SSID)ND Lodge Staff
    SecurityWPA-Personal (WPA2), with the staff Wi-Fi password from Part 0
    Guest NetworkOff
    SSID BroadcastOff (hidden: only people who know the name can join)
  3. Click Apply.
Why a staff network?

If netflexhq.com or the internet is ever down, staff can still join ND Lodge Staff and reach the controller to fix things. Don't give its password to residents.

Check

On your phone's Wi-Fi list you see ND Lodge Wi-Fi. Don't join it yet: it has no sign-in page until Part 9, so for now anyone could use it freely.

Part 6 done: both networks created.

Part 7 · 15 minutes

Two accounts for Netflex

Netflex needs permission to switch residents' phones on and off. You give it two limited accounts inside the controller. Neither can change your settings or passwords.

A. The hotspot operator (switches phones on)

  1. In the controller, open Hotspot Manager. Depending on the version it's in the top menu, the left menu, or under the site's menu. If you can't see it, type hotspot in the controller's search.
  2. Click Operators, then + Add.
  3. Name: netflex-link. Password: the hotspot operator password from Part 0. Site: ND Lodge. Leave the rest. Click Apply / Save.

B. The Open API app (switches phones off early, sees who's online)

  1. Go back to the main controller (click Global View or the controller name at the top-left if needed).
  2. Open Settings → Platform Integration → Open API.
  3. Click + Add New App. App name: Netflex. Mode: Client. Role: Administrator. Site: ND Lodge. Click Apply.
  4. The app now shows a Client ID and a Client Secret. Leave this page open: you'll copy them in Part 10.
  5. On the same page, find the link to the API documentation (it may say "View API documentation" or show a document icon). Open it.
  6. In the documentation, use the search or browse to Hotspot / Authorized clients, and find the call that unauthorizes or de-authorizes a client. It looks like a line starting with POST and containing /openapi/v1/.
  7. Take a screenshot of that line and send it to Netflex (WhatsApp is fine: it isn't a password).
Can't find it?

Send a screenshot of the API documentation's list of sections instead. Everything still works without it, except cutting off access early (for example after a refund).

Part 7 done: operator "netflex-link" created; Open API app created; screenshot sent.

Part 8 · 5 minutes · the owner

Register the controller in the office

Where: netflexhq.com/admin, on the laptop or any computer, signed in as the owner

  1. Sign in at netflexhq.com/admin with the owner's email, password and authenticator code.
  2. Click Network in the left menu (the globe icon).
  3. Click Add Omada controller (top-right).
  4. Name: leave Omada controller. Equipment: 3× EAP115 v4. Click Add controller.
  5. Under Equipment you now see Omada controller with Lodge link: Not paired. That's expected.
  6. Find Omada portal address. It looks like https://netflexhq.com/connect/omada/… followed by a long code. Click the copy icon next to it.
  7. If you're on another computer, send this address to the laptop: paste it into an email to yourself, or write it down carefully. (It isn't secret.)
Check

The Network page shows the Omada controller and its portal address.

Part 8 done: controller added in the office; portal address copied.

Part 9 · 20 minutes

Turn on the sign-in page

This makes every phone that joins ND Lodge Wi-Fi see the Netflex page first, where residents enter a code or buy a plan.

Where: Omada Controller → Settings → Authentication → Portal

A. Create the portal

  1. Click Create New Portal (or + Add).
  2. Fill it in:
    SettingValue
    Portal NameNetflex
    SSID & NetworkTick ND Lodge Wi-Fi only (not ND Lodge Staff)
    Authentication TypeExternal Portal Server
    Custom Portal ServerChoose URL and paste the Omada portal address from Part 8
    HTTPS RedirectionOn
    Landing PageThe Original URL
  3. Click Apply.

B. Let people reach the payment pages before paying

  1. Still under Authentication, open Portal → your Netflex portal → Access Control (in some versions it's a separate tab called Access Control under Authentication).
  2. Turn on Pre-Authentication Access.
  3. Add each of these as type URL, one at a time, clicking + Add for each:
    #URL (type exactly)
    1netflexhq.com
    2checkout.paystack.com
    3js.paystack.co
    4api.paystack.co
    5paystack.com
  4. Click Apply.
About card payments

Some banks show their own security page during a card payment. If a payment on the lodge Wi-Fi gets stuck on a bank page, the resident can pay on mobile data instead. Tell Netflex which bank, and we can add it here.

Part 9 done: portal created; 5 payment addresses added.

Part 10 · 25 minutes

Install the lodge link

The lodge link is a small Netflex program. It lets netflexhq.com tell the controller "switch this phone on for 24 hours", even though the lodge has no public internet address.

A. Download it

  1. On the laptop, sign in at netflexhq.com/admin (the owner's account) → Network.
  2. Under Omada controller, click Pair lodge link. In the panel that opens, click download it here. Close the panel for now with the ×.
  3. Open the Downloads folder, right-click lodge-link.zip → Extract All…
  4. In the box, replace the folder with C:\NetFlex and click Extract. You now have the folder C:\NetFlex\lodge-link.

B. Open PowerShell as administrator

  1. Click Start, type powershell. On the right, click Run as administrator. Click Yes.
  2. Type this and press Enter: cd C:\NetFlex\lodge-link

C. Enter the settings

  1. Type node lodge-link.mjs setup and press Enter. It asks 6 questions, one by one. Type the answer and press Enter after each:
    QuestionAnswer
    NetFlex addressJust press Enter (keeps https://netflexhq.com)
    Omada controller addressJust press Enter (keeps https://127.0.0.1:8043)
    Hotspot operator nameJust press Enter (keeps netflex-link)
    Hotspot operator passwordType the hotspot operator password from Part 7A
    Open API client IDCopy the Client ID from Part 7B and paste it (right-click pastes in PowerShell)
    Open API client secretCopy and paste the Client Secret from Part 7B
Saved C:\NetFlex\lodge-link\lodge-link.json. Next: node lodge-link.mjs check

D. Pair it with the office

  1. In the office (Network page), click Pair lodge link again, then Make a pairing code. You see a code like K7QH-3MZP. It works once, for 30 minutes.
  2. In PowerShell, type node lodge-link.mjs pair, a space, then the code, and press Enter. For example: node lodge-link.mjs pair K7QH-3MZP
Paired with "Omada controller". Next: node lodge-link.mjs check

Click Done in the office panel.

E. Check everything answers

  1. Type node lodge-link.mjs check and press Enter.
Controller OK (Omada 5.15.8) Switch devices off early: not set up (Open API) List connected devices: yes Server OK (paired)
Check

You see Controller OK and Server OK. "Switch devices off early: not set up" is normal until Netflex sends one extra setting from your Part 7 screenshot.

F. Make it start by itself

  1. Type powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\install.ps1 and press Enter.
Installed and started 'NetFlex Lodge Link'. It starts by itself whenever the computer turns on.
Check

In the office, refresh the Network page. Within a minute, Lodge link says Online, and the Omada controller shows Working after you click Check now.

Part 10 done: lodge link installed, paired and online.

Part 11 · 20 minutes

Test with a real phone

Prove that a code really turns the Wi-Fi on, before any resident pays.

A. Make a free test code (owner, in the office)

  1. Office → Vouchers → Make vouchers.
  2. Plan: Daily. How many: 1. Note: setup test. Click Make codes.
  3. Write the code down. It's only shown once.

B. Use it on the test phone

  1. On the test phone, turn mobile data off.
  2. In Wi-Fi settings, join ND Lodge Wi-Fi.
  3. Within a few seconds, a sign-in page should pop up showing ND Lodge. If nothing pops up, open the browser and go to any website, like google.com: you'll be sent to the sign-in page.
  4. Tap I have a voucher, type the code, tap Connect.
  5. Wait up to a minute. The page says You're connected.
  6. Open google.com and a video on YouTube to check the internet really works.

C. Check the time is right

  1. In the Omada Controller on the laptop, open Hotspot Manager → Authorized Clients.
  2. Find the test phone. Look at its expiry or valid until time.
  3. Take a screenshot and send it to Netflex.
Check

The expiry is about 24 hours from now (tomorrow, around the same time).

If the expiry looks wrong (1970, a few minutes, or weeks away)

Don't worry: it's one setting. Send the screenshot to Netflex and we'll send back the exact line to change.

D. Check the other floors

  1. Walk to each floor with the test phone. Open a few websites in each hallway and in one room at the far end of each floor.
  2. Note any room where it's slow or drops out.
Part 11 done: test phone online; expiry screenshot sent; all floors checked.

Part 12 · 5 minutes

Finish and keep it running

Send Netflex

  • Screenshot of the unauthorize call (Part 7B)
  • Screenshot of the test phone's expiry (Part 11C)
  • Screenshot of Omada Devices, showing all 3 access points Connected
  • Any rooms with a weak signal (Part 11D)

Every day

  • If the owner gets an email from Netflex saying "Wi-Fi equipment isn't answering", check the laptop first.
